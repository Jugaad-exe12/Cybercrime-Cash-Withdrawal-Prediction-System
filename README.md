### SIH-26184

> **Development of a Predictive Analytics Framework for Cybercrime Complaints to Forecast Likely Cash Withdrawal Locations in Advance, Enabling Generation of Actionable Intelligence for Timely and Proactive Cybercrime Intervention.**

## 📌 Overview

Cybercrime and financial fraud generate a large number of complaints every day, making it difficult to manually identify patterns and predict where fraudulent funds may be withdrawn.

This Project proposes a predictive analytics platform that analyzes historical cybercrime and financial patterns to identify **geographic areas with elevated risk of future cash withdrawals**.

The system combines **data analytics, machine learning, geospatial visualization, and alert generation** to transform raw complaint data into actionable intelligence.

### 🚀 Key Capabilities

- 📊 **Predictive Analytics** — Analyze historical patterns and estimate geographic risk.
- 🗺️ **GIS Risk Heatmap** — Visualize high-, medium-, and low-risk areas.
- 🚨 **Alert System** — Generate alerts when risk exceeds defined thresholds.
- 👮 **Law-Enforcement Dashboard** — Provide authorized users with geographic and analytical intelligence.
- 🔍 **Explainable Predictions** — Show the factors contributing to a risk prediction.
- 📈 **Trend Analysis** — Analyze patterns across time, location, and fraud categories.

### 🎯 Objective

The primary objective is to move from a **reactive approach** to a **proactive, data-driven approach** to financial cybercrime by helping authorized stakeholders identify potential risk areas earlier and prioritize their response.

## ⚠️ Disclaimer

This project is developed as a **Smart India Hackathon (SIH-26184) prototype** for demonstration and research purposes.

- The prototype may use **synthetic, simulated, or anonymized data**.
- It is **not connected to real government, I4C, NCRP, banking, ATM, or citizen databases**.
- Any locations, risk scores, predictions, or visualizations shown in the prototype are **demonstrative and should not be treated as real-world intelligence**.
- The system is designed as a **decision-support tool**, not an automated law-enforcement system.
- Predictions should not be interpreted as proof that a crime will occur or that any individual is involved in criminal activity.
- A production implementation would require appropriate **authorization, data governance, security controls, validation, and integration with relevant institutional systems**.

> **From cybercrime complaints to predictive geographic intelligence — enabling earlier, data-driven and human-supervised intervention.**
